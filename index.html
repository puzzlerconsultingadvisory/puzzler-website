The Operator Gap

The strategy is rarely the problem. Operating it is.

Most engagements start at a threshold — a new leader, an audit finding, a capital project turning harder than planned, a funding mechanism that just got more complicated.

Between the plan and the people who have to run it sits the operator gap: the governance, funding discipline, and capacity that a strategy assumes but rarely builds.

Organizations don’t fail because their strategy is wrong. They fail because no one stays in the hole with them long enough to get them out. We do the work alongside the people responsible for it, not at a distance from them.

Seven steps · Three phases

The Puzzler Method

How a practitioner takes an organization doing meaningful work from a scattered pile of pieces to a finished picture. Select a step to see what it does to the frame.

01 Start With The Edge

Phase 1 · Start With The Edge Steps 1–3

  1. Trace the outside first. Before anything moves, we establish the boundaries of the engagement — mission, money, mandate, and the people accountable for each — and how the corners relate.

  2. Close the perimeter. Governance, decision rights, and the operating rhythm become a stable frame that every later piece has to fit inside.

  3. Organize the interior into working groups — programs, funding streams, functions, and people — without pulling anything out of the picture.

Phase 2 · Sort and Build Steps 4–6

  1. Clarify the relationships that matter. Decision-ready briefs, clear paths, and visible connections so leadership can act without losing what matters in translation.

  2. Stop at visible checkpoints. Pausing on purpose is how a team keeps its judgment — and how leadership sees progress instead of motion.

  3. Restore the whole picture. When one problem dominates, the rest of the organization drifts. We bring every area back to equal visibility before choosing the next move.

Phase 3 · Activate and Sustain Step 7

  1. Find the unstable connection, correct it, and return it to a durable state — the compliance, monitoring, and stewardship discipline that determines whether you keep what you win.

Build It to Hold

Five practices. One operating discipline.

The work nonprofits, small businesses, and organizations doing meaningful work bring to us — across every funding mechanism. What ties it together is simple: we do the work alongside the people responsible for it, not at a distance from them.

Organizational restructuring and transition support

Reorganizations, mergers, leadership transitions, and program portfolio rationalizations. The structural work that determines whether an organization can do its job for the next decade — built on enterprise reorganizations led at federal scale.

Funding strategy across every mechanism

Grants, cooperative agreements, contracts, and gifts — and the compliance regimes that come with each. Most mid-sized nonprofits, small businesses, and organizations doing meaningful work run all four at once. We’re built to work the whole portfolio.

Capacity building that holds

Operational governance, financial planning and analysis, shared-services design, and the unglamorous infrastructure that determines whether mission gets executed. The kind of capacity that doesn’t unravel when the next transformative event arrives.

Program effectiveness and accountability

Compliance monitoring and program review for grants and contracts. Site visits, subrecipient monitoring, and the operational disciplines that produce strong year-over-year outcomes — not just clean audits.

Leadership and workforce development

Executive coaching, succession planning, tailored workshops, and workforce training. The customized development of leaders and teams who can actually do the work.

A note from Mark

People remember FEMA for the emergencies. I remember it for what came after.

As an emergency manager, I learned that the work that really matters isn’t the response — it’s helping people find their way back to a new normal. Not their old life. A new one. You can’t hand someone a plan and call that help. You have to get down where they are, see what they see, and stay with them until the ground feels steady again.

I’ve spent two decades building, modernizing, and stewarding the systems nonprofits, small businesses, and organizations doing meaningful work rely on. What ties those chapters together is simple. Organizations don’t fail because their strategy is wrong. They fail because no one stays in the hole with them long enough to get them out. Puzzler was built to be the partner that stays.

Every engagement begins the same way: with a conversation. Tell us where you are, where you’re trying to get to, and what’s in the way. We’ll get in the hole with you — and help you find your way to a new normal.

— Mark
